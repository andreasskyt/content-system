# Packaging strategy

Packaging is not decoration applied after the video. It is the product the
viewer actually buys. The video is what they get after they buy.

**Do not ask "does this look good?" Ask "would the right viewer understand the
premise in one second and feel compelled to find out what happens next?"**

---

## Step 1 — Strip the transcript

From the transcript, extract and write down before anything else:

| Field | What it means |
|---|---|
| **One idea** | The single claim the video makes. One sentence. If you need "and", you have two videos. |
| **Promise** | What the viewer walks away able to do / know / avoid. |
| **Who it's for** | Business owner drowning in ops? Developer? Agency owner? Be specific — the wrong viewer clicking is worse than no click. |
| **Proof** | The concrete thing shown — a system, a number, a client result, a screen. This becomes the artifact. |
| **The gap** | What the viewer currently believes vs. what the video reveals. The gap IS the thumbnail. |
| **Emotional beat** | frustration · shock · relief · greed · fear-of-missing · vindication. Picks the photo. |

If the transcript has no gap, say so. A video with no gap gets a descriptive
package, not a curiosity one, and it will underperform — that is a content
problem, not a packaging problem, and [YOUR_NAME] should hear it.

## Step 2 — Five hypotheses, not five skins

Each concept must differ in **what it argues**, not in colour or crop. If two
concepts would be described the same way out loud, one of them is wasted.

Force spread across these angles — pick 5 distinct ones:

1. **The mistake** — show the wrong thing, mark it wrong. (stamp, red, frustrated face)
2. **The number** — one stat so specific it demands explanation.
3. **The comparison** — this vs. that, before vs. after, cheap vs. right.
4. **The reveal** — show the thing normally hidden (the backend, the invoice, the real cost).
5. **The contrarian** — state the opposite of consensus, deadpan.
6. **The outcome** — the end state the viewer wants, made concrete.
7. **The object** — no face, one arresting object. Pattern break in a face-heavy feed.

## Step 3 — Score before rendering

Kill concepts on paper. Score each 1–5, drop anything under 18:

- **Instant read** — understood in <1s at 320px wide
- **Curiosity** — creates a question the title doesn't answer
- **Specificity** — a real number/name/object beats a vague claim
- **Honesty** — the video actually delivers this
- **Distinctiveness** — doesn't look like the other 11 results in the feed

## Step 4 — Title and thumbnail are one unit

The thumbnail shows X, the title expands the context. **Never say the same
thing twice** — duplicated information is wasted real estate.

Bad pair: thumbnail says "NEVER DO THIS" / title says "Never do this with dashboards"
Good pair: thumbnail says "NEVER DO THIS" / title says "Why your vibe-coded dashboard breaks in week 3"

Generate the title *with* its concept, as a pair. Never produce a detached list
of titles and a detached list of images.

## Step 5 — Feed test, then fix one thing

Look at `_feed-test.png` (all concepts at real feed width). For each, ask:

- Can I read the text without leaning in?
- Do I know what the video is about, or only that it looks nice?
- Does the face read as an emotion, or as a blob?
- Would it survive next to 11 competing thumbnails?

Then fix **the single biggest weakness**, not five small ones. If the weakness
is strategic (weak idea), go back to Step 2 — do not fix it with typography.

`references/performance.md`. Read that file at the start of every run. What
